I am an applied microeconomist working in urban, public, and health economics. My research asks how housing, homelessness, and health programs affect the people they serve and the neighborhoods around them, and what those programs cost.

I use administrative records, Medicaid claims, and housing transaction data with quasi-experimental and spatial methods: difference-in-differences, hedonic pricing models, and spatial econometrics. I received my Ph.D. in Economics from the University of New Mexico in 2024.

Research

Homelessness and housing policy

How shelters, housing programs, and encampment enforcement after Grants Pass v. Johnson affect property values, rents, and local crime. Settings include Seattle, Denver, Los Angeles, and the Built for Zero communities.

Health and substance use treatment

Costs and outcomes of peer support and recovery services for substance use disorders, including their link to Medicaid billing.

Coastal flooding and housing markets

How flooding changes housing market liquidity, and how tidal flooding and historical redlining shape access to emergency care in coastal Virginia.

The safety net

How state Earned Income Tax Credit supplements change what households spend their refunds on.
